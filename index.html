Chapter 1

A name that belongs to a place

A Geographical Indication (GI) tag says a product's qualities come from where it is made. Think Champagne, or Darjeeling Tea.

The rule

Only producers from that place may use the name. Tea grown in Assam can't be sold as Darjeeling.

The count

As of 31 December 2025, India has registered 684 of these. Each one is a recipe, a crop or a craft that a place calls its own.

Chapter 2

It began with tea

Each bar is one year of applications. The line climbs to the full register.

2003

Darjeeling Tea was the first ever application, filed on 27 October 2003.

2006 to 2010

The first wave: 160 filings in five years, mostly textiles and crafts.

2022

129 filings in a single year. Almost 1 in 5 of every GI India has.

Since 2023

The drop is not a slowdown. This register lists only approved GIs, and newer applications are still waiting in the queue.

Chapter 3

Mostly made by hand

Line all 684 up on one shelf, sorted by what they are.

Handicraft

375 are handmade: sarees, toys, pottery, metalwork. More than half.

Agricultural

225 are grown: rice, mangoes, chillies, tea. About 1 in 3.

The rest

54 foods, 27 manufactured goods, and just 3 natural: two marbles and a sandstone.

Chapter 4

Where they come from

Each tile is a state, placed where it sits on the map. Darker means more GIs. The strip at the bottom shows its mix of crafts and crops.

The leaders

Uttar Pradesh (78) and Tamil Nadu (74) lead the country.

A craft state

In Uttar Pradesh, 56 of 78 are handmade: Banaras brocade, Lucknow chikan, Moradabad brass.

A farm state

Maharashtra is the opposite. 37 of its 52 are crops: Alphonso, Nashik grapes, Sangli turmeric.

A caution

Jharkhand has just 1. A count shows who applied, not whose culture is richer.

About this data

Every number on this page comes from one public register. Here is what it does and doesn't say.

32

entries are logo registrations of a GI that already exists, so there are about 652 distinct products.

17

GIs are shared by more than one state (like Basmati and Phulkari). They are left off the state maps.

Filed

Dates are filing dates, not approval dates. Approval can take years.

1

date is a typo in the register (Uttarakhand Bedu, "2027"), so it is left off the timeline.
